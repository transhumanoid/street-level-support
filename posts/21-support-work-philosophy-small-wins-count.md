# Small Wins Count

Notes on why good support notices the small steps, and doesn't wait for the big change before saying "well done"

A lot of support is built around big goals. Get housed. Get clean. Get a job. Get well. Those goals matter. But for most people, they are a long way off on a bad day. If the only thing that counts is the finish line, most days look like failure.

Street-level support works better when the small steps count too.

## What a small win looks like

Small wins are easy to miss if you are not looking for them:

- Someone who usually avoids the service drops in to say hello.
- A person keeps a phone charged so they can be reached.
- Someone turns up to an appointment, even late.
- A person tells you the truth about a bad week, instead of hiding it.

None of these will show up in a big report. All of them are real progress. They are the steps the bigger changes are built on.

## Checking the opposite

Our usual habit applies here: when everyone says "only real change counts", check the opposite.

The mainstream idea is that praising or rewarding small steps is soft, or that it "lets people off the hook". But there is good public evidence pointing the other way. An approach called contingency management, where people get small, quick, concrete rewards for meeting clear goals (often a clean test result for stimulant use), has been studied for decades and is widely recognised as one of the more effective tools for that kind of problem. The details vary, and it is not magic. But the basic idea holds up: people respond to progress being noticed, quickly and reliably.

Punishing every slip tends to teach people to hide. Noticing every step tends to teach people to keep going.

## What this looks like in practice

You do not need a formal program to use the idea:

- **Name it out loud.** "You came back. That's good." It takes five seconds.
- **Make the reward quick.** A coffee, a meal, a lift somewhere. A reward that arrives weeks later loses most of its pull.
- **Keep the goal small and clear.** "Come by on Thursday" is easier to win than "sort your life out".
- **Don't take it back after a slip.** A bad day does not cancel the good ones. Start again from where the person is.

## Why this matters

People who have been through the system often carry a long list of things they "failed" at. Every missed appointment, every relapse, every lost tenancy is written down somewhere. Very few of their small wins are written down anywhere.

Good support turns that around. It keeps a record, even just in conversation, of what is going right. Over time, that record becomes something a person can stand on.

## What you can take from this

If you support anyone, whether at work, in your family, or as a friend, try this for a week:

1. Notice one small step each time you see them, and say it.
2. If you can, make something good happen soon after, even something tiny.
3. When they slip, skip the lecture and ask what would help tomorrow.

Big change is usually just a lot of small wins that nobody gave up on.
